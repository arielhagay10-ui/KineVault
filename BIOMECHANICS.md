# Biomechanical classification

**Muscles** describe tissue involvement and have primary, secondary or stabilizer roles. **Joints** describe anatomical articulations or regions. **Joint actions** describe movements at those joints; each action belongs to a joint. An exercise may have several reviewed actions with primary, secondary or stabilization roles. Do not infer actions solely from muscles or rig poses.

**Movement patterns** are broad exercise categories such as horizontal push or squat. They are independent of joint actions. **Planes of motion** describe the broad geometric plane and may be multiplanar. **Exercise families** group a movement concept; **variations** are distinct exercises linked to a family and, where applicable, to a parent variation. A variation may classify every field differently from its parent.

**Resistance profile** describes how external mechanical challenge changes through the usable concentric range: ascending, descending, bell-shaped, relatively constant, variable/complex, or unknown. Peak position is beginning, middle, end, multiple, or unknown. Machine geometry, cable direction, setup, body position and technique can change the classification. Use `unknown` and reviewer notes when evidence is insufficient. Do not substitute muscle length bias for external resistance profile.
