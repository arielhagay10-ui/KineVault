# Equipment model sources

`lat-pulldown.v1.glb` is original KineVault equipment geometry, generated with
Blender from `scripts/equipment/build_lat_pulldown.py`. It includes no external
meshes or textures and contains no human anatomy model.

The other 22 versioned equipment GLBs and 23 picker thumbnails are original
KineVault geometry generated by `scripts/equipment/build_equipment.py`. The
transparent WebP picker files are resized by
`scripts/equipment/optimize_thumbnails.mjs`. These assets likewise contain no
external meshes, textures, or human model.

`shoulder-press-machine.v2.glb` is original primitive geometry with a compact
silver frame, black upholstery, and wide and swiveling neutral grips on each
independent lever. Its dimensions come from `src/lib/motion/shoulder-press.ts`.
The visible neutral-grip swivel keeps its shaft facing front to back through
the shared lever arc. This is a functional design addition, not a mechanism
confirmed in the reference photo.

`leg-press.v2.glb` uses the original KineVault frame with a footplate tilted
20 degrees from vertical. Its working stroke and sole contact geometry come
from `src/lib/motion/leg-press.ts`, calibrated to the existing fixed-length rig
and hip, knee and ankle limits. This is a functional design correction, not a
manufacturer-specific reproduction. The back pad and its connected supports
clear the default torso without moving the seated figure.

Human anatomy remains the existing bundled Z-Anatomy asset and its prepared
preview variants. See `../z-anatomy/ATTRIBUTION.md` for the separate anatomy
credits and licenses. Generated page illustrations are not anatomy assets.

`t-bar-row-machine.v1.glb` and its picker thumbnail are original KineVault
geometry from `scripts/equipment/build_t_bar_row.py`, modelled from the user's
machine reference. No external meshes, textures, logos or anatomy are included.

`chest-press-machine.v1.glb` and its picker thumbnail are original geometry from
`scripts/equipment/build_chest_press.py`, informed by the user's handle reference.
The bottom pivots, converging travel and handle bearings are an inferred teaching
model, not a reconstruction of the photographed machine's hidden linkage.
