# Current workshop

Updated 5 October 2026. Source and visible controls are authoritative.

First-time creators see a four-step tutorial with fixed instructions and a moving Kine mascot. The editor dims around a clear, outlined spotlight on the equipment choices, start/finish buttons, Play, and name/save fields. Kine independently chooses an up, down, left or right pointing pose to suit available space and avoids both the target and instructions. The text panel stays beside the figure on the right of the desktop preview, uses the top center on tablets, and reserves a bottom area on mobile. Controls and editor shortcuts stay usable during the tour. Spotlight placement follows scrolling and resizing, clips to visible controls, and supports mobile scrolling and Hebrew layout. Reduced motion disables mascot travel. Finish tutorial, Close tutorial or Escape dismisses it without changing the current editing step. Progress is account-scoped and can be replayed. Switching to advanced controls offers Return to this tip. Quick create shows one task at a time; grips and equipment details stay in disclosures. Save privately is in the final step.

The viewer uses the 3D simulator. When WebGL 2 is unavailable or loading fails, it offers Retry preview and a text scene summary; editing and saving remain available. On mobile, scroll between the preview and editing controls; playback controls retain their natural height. Leave machine selects the freed figure for placement.

Quick create guides equipment, start/finish, preview, then name/save. Every scene uses Edit start, Edit finish and Match return to start. Quick create has no machine travel percentages, equipment-specific movement panels, range presets or linked fit scaling. Advanced editing exposes tool tabs, detailed poses and timeline. One scene owner coordinates history, gestures, private autosave and recovery. Extra metadata remains optional. Precise placement and Drag options keep numeric transforms and movement settings closed until requested. Cable attachment buttons, hand choices and wrist grip presets appear for the selected cable in both Quick create and Contacts.

Choose equipment offers common weights, cables and supports as shortcuts that add to the current scene. View all equipment and search expose the complete catalog. Recovery and shortcuts lives inside More. Opening a dropdown closes the previous one; header menus dismiss when clicking outside.

Reset all joints clears the editable joints in the selected pose, or every pose when that scope is selected, in one Undo step. Equipment-controlled joints remain in contact. Right-drag orbits the camera during mouse editing; left-drag continues moving the selected item.

Hand and foot posing advisories appear as compact amber indicators inside the canvas without resizing it. Hover or keyboard focus reveals details after dragging; screen readers receive the advisory when the gesture ends.

Free cable movement creates a low pulley, or replaces a selected row setup while preserving authored poses, the selected attachment and other equipment. Free body releases the row's seated contacts while keeping the assembly and grip; hips, knees, torso, feet and body placement remain editable for deadlifts and other movements. In Move with mouse, dragging an attachment or its round grab button moves the held hands without moving the tower. Arrow keys also move the handle; Escape cancels and Undo restores the whole gesture. Pose hands and feet exposes individual limb buttons instead. Cable rows and free towers share all seven attachment choices, including the wide-grip lat bar and two-arm angled bar; seated rows retain seat and foot support when changing attachments. New towers face the figure. Position shortcuts place a held tower in front, behind or beside the figure; turn buttons and precise coordinates remain independent of the hand attachment. Adding a cable row places it without engaging seated contacts; Use this machine in Advanced Contacts explicitly enables them. Examples still create complete linked movements. Edit start and Edit finish select the corresponding saved pose before editing; Match return to start closes the loop. Exact Start/Finish preview moments also select their matching pose.

Saved keyframes describe body poses and independently animated equipment. Placement stays static until animation is enabled. Held weights drive the arms; releasing them restores manual posing. Seating, machine support, elbow locks, foot contacts, cuffs and frontal-plane constraints survive validated saves. Classifications remain distinct from approximate motion solving.

The shared playback clock updates labels and sliders at a lower frequency. Canvas frames solve the figure before equipment/contact consumers. Camera-only frames reuse poses. Viewers share immutable prepared anatomy geometry while keeping skeletons, highlights and materials separate. Closing the final viewer releases geometry GPU buffers.

Private saves run serially with persistent UUIDs. Owner-scoped recovery preserves unsaved/offline scenes. Metadata forms warn before discarding changes. Render jobs carry claim IDs so expired workers cannot complete/fail another claim. Capture and encoding have deadlines.
